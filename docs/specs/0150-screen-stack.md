## Problem Statement

The operator works in one terminal screen at a time. Today, when a screen stands over another - the Key guide over a streaming Live view, a panel over the Main view, the override panel over the Decision modal - the screen beneath keeps living. Its one-second reads of the Agent's pane keep running and keep asking herdr through the plane's runner, its spinner faces keep stepping every hundred milliseconds, and every one of those ticks commits at the root of the plane's single tree, so the machine re-lays out the screen the operator reads as well as the screen the operator does not see. The operator sees none of this work: the screen above covers the screen below. The machine pays for it all: a wake, a command to herdr, a pass over the whole tree, on every tick, for as long as the operator reads the screen above. On a loaded machine those background passes interleave with the screen the operator is reading, and the test suite that measures that screen pays the same interleaving in its frame waits. Meanwhile the plane's own loops - the Observation cycle, the refresh schedule, the Handoff dispatch - keep working whether or not any screen shows their results, and they must: the factory does not wait for the operator.

## Solution

The plane holds exactly one active screen at a time. The Main view is the bottom of the screen stack, and the screens that open above it - the Consultation launcher and the Replacement Consultation, the Key guide, the Message view, the Decision modal, the Missing modal, the ticket and Consultation close and safety, recovery, force, and delete panels, the repository select and repository init panels, the override panel, and the Live view - sit on top of it in the order they opened. Opening a screen suspends the screen beneath it: the suspended screen unmounts, so its timers stop, its spinner faces stop, it sends no read through the runner, and it holds no key subscription. It keeps its place and its facts in the stack. Closing the active screen resumes the screen beneath it: the screen remounts, it reads the factory's facts fresh from the store, it takes its navigation, its presented lines, and its drafts back from the stack's preserved facts for it, and it stands on the same frame it was suspended on, with no re-pop-in, until its next tick moves it. The modes of the detail pane - the response editor and the Agent terminal - are not screens of the stack: they belong to the Main view and suspend with it, and their drafts and their editing position are preserved facts of the Main view. The plane's loops run through every transition, untouched by the stack: the screen is what the operator sees, and the loops are what the factory does. The operator's keys route exactly as they do today: the active screen owns the keys, the suspended screens claim none, and the emergency exit stands in every mode.

## User Stories

1. As an operator, I want the Live view's stream to stop its one-second reads while I read the Key guide over it, so that the Agent's pane is not read for a screen I do not see.
2. As an operator, I want to close the guide and find the Live view standing on the same stream lines I left, so that a resume does not clear what I was reading.
3. As an operator, I want the Consultation detail's body to stop its one-second read while a panel stands over the Main view, so that my reads do not run herdr's scroll in the neighbour pane behind a panel (the visible-screen read, ADR 0140, runs only for the screen that is visible).
4. As an operator, I want the spinner faces of a suspended screen to stand still, so that a screen I do not see owes no motion.
5. As an operator, I want the Consultation detail to come back with my scroll position, my selection, and the same lines when I close the panel over it, so that I can keep reading where I left off.
6. As an operator, I want to open Help in the middle of a sentence in the response editor and come back to my draft, my caret, and my undo, so that the shared standard's rule for Help holds for every screen, not only the ones that stay mounted.
7. As an operator, I want the launcher's draft to stand when I look up a key in the middle of a launch, so that the guide is safe to open on any screen.
8. As an operator, I want to open the override panel over the Decision modal or the Live view and come back to the same row and the same sub-mode I had, so that editing a route does not move my place in the screen I came from.
9. As an operator, I want the emergency exit to work from every screen, suspended or active, so that the plane's one always-available key is never blocked by a screen above it.
10. As an operator, I want the factory to keep working while I read any screen: the Observation cycle keeps reclaiming, settling, and topping up behind every overlay, so that the list I return to already shows what happened while I was away.
11. As an operator, I want a screen I resume to stand at full size, without the pop-in of an opening, so that returning to a screen reads as a return, not a new opening.
12. As an operator, I want my selection, my list filters, my section expansions, and my grouping folds on the Main view to stand when I come back from any overlay, so that my view of the factory is mine and not the defaults.
13. As an operator, I want the Work queue item's detail to come back with its scroll, so that a long record does not reset under me when a panel interrupts it.
14. As an operator, I want the standing facts - the queue pause's lamp, the Auto-handoff mode's cell, the counts - to show their current state on every screen I resume, so that a suspended screen never stands a stale state of the factory.
15. As an operator, I want a message the plane wrote while I read the guide to show on the screen I return to, so that I do not miss what the factory said while I was away.
16. As an operator, I want a Consultation that closes itself by an automatic decision while I read the guide to show its closed record when I return, so that the machine's work is visible at my return, not hidden behind the screen I was reading.
17. As an operator, I want the Live view to close instead of resuming when the ticket moved to its next work cycle while I was away, so that I do not return to a stream of a passage the plane has already left (ADR 0110).
18. As an operator, I want the guide opened over the guide to update its mode in place, so that the same screen never stacks on itself.
19. As a machine, I want a suspended screen to hold no timer, so that no one-second tick wakes me while the operator reads the screen above.
20. As a machine, I want a suspended screen to send no command through the runner, so that no herdr pane read runs for a screen no one sees.
21. As a machine, I want a suspended screen's spinner faces to owe no interval, so that the hundred-millisecond face step stops with the screen.
22. As a machine, I want no background commit to interleave with the active screen's repaint, so that the frame I draw for the operator is the frame that stands, and the rig's stability waits stop racing a tick.
23. As a machine, I want to lay out one screen at a time, so that a tick commits the screen the operator sees, not the trees of every screen.
24. As a machine, I want the plane's loops to keep their cadence through every screen transition, so that the factory's work never waits on the operator's screen.
25. As a test suite, I want to read the runner's command list across a window longer than the suspended screen's tick period and see it stand, so that "a suspended screen runs nothing" is a measured fact.
26. As a test suite, I want the painted frame to stand unchanged across that window, so that a suspended screen leaves no trace on the screen.
27. As a test suite, I want the suspended screen's key wrapper off the key bus while it is suspended, and back on it with the same identity when it resumes, so that the rig's base snapshot keeps its meaning across a suspend of the screen it snapshotted.
28. As a test suite, I want the resumed screen's first tick to land and move the frame, so that a resume is proven alive, not merely un-suspended.
29. As a test suite, I want my existing frame walks - the Consultation frame walks that open the launcher, the guide, and the Consultation panels; the handoff frame walks that open the Live view and the override panel and close them on the cancel control; the auto-mode walks that open the Missing modal, the ticket close, and the decision surfaces; the action bar walks over every mode; the main view walk over the guide's window - to keep passing without re-pinning, because the visible output and the key routing stand as they are.
30. As a test suite, I want the stale-key guard to keep its answer: a key landed in the window between a closed surface's last frame and its key release claims nothing and runs no behavior.
31. As a test suite, I want a suspended screen's draft to read back through the painted frame after a resume, so that the preservation rule is asserted from the screen, not from the state that holds it.
32. As a test suite, I want a screen whose stored fact no longer stands to close instead of resuming, asserted through the frame it goes away on, so that the resume rule is measured on the screen.

## Implementation Decisions

**The screen stack module.** The plane gains one new module, the screen stack, owned by the plane's shell. It owns three things and nothing else: the stack's record (the ordered suspended screens, the Main view at the bottom, the active screen at the top), the transitions (open, close, replace), and the contract that a suspended screen runs nothing. Its interface names, for each screen: how the screen presents itself to the stack while it is active, the screen's preserved facts (the record the stack stores on suspend and hands back on resume), and the screen's key dispatch slot (the named surface under which the screen's keys register with the shared dispatch module). The stack changes which screens stand and when; it never changes how a screen presents, how its controls dispatch, or what its controls mean. The stack composes the screens; it re-implements none of their controls, so the shared control library's rule stands: a screen keeps its domain validation, its drafts, and its Agent actions behind the shared modules (ADR 0014).

**What a screen is, and what is not.** The screens of the stack are the Main view and the screens that open above it: the Consultation launcher (with the Replacement Consultation as the same screen in a second context), the Key guide, the Message view, the Decision modal, the Missing modal, the ticket close, the Consultation close, safety, recovery, force, and delete panels, the repository select, the repository init, the override panel, and the Live view. The response editor and the Agent terminal are modes of the Main view's detail pane: they are not screens of the stack, they suspend with the Main view, and their facts are preserved facts of the Main view. The Live view's sub-modes - its stream, its decision, its missing face - are not screen transitions: they switch in place on the same surface, one pop-in per opening, the way the shared chrome already decides (ADR 0040). The stack opens, closes, and replaces screens; it never sees a sub-mode.

**The transitions.** Open: the stack puts the opened screen on top and suspends the screen that was active. An open of the screen that is already active updates that screen's fact in place (the guide's captured Interaction mode), never a second entry in the stack. Open of a screen the stack already holds at a lower level is refused: the stack holds one entry per screen, and the legal configurations the plane runs today (one of launcher or a panel over the Main view, the override over a screen it can return to, a utility over any screen) stand as the open rule's facts, each transition carrying the facts it carries today: the override's return-to screen, the Live view's ticket identity and work cycle, the launcher's replacement context, the repository init's queue of marked repositories. Close: the stack removes the active screen and resumes the screen beneath it. Replace: a close of one screen immediately followed, in the same transition pass, by the open of a different screen (the decision route confirm that opens the routed ticket's Live view; the repository select's confirm that opens the repository init) swaps the top without resuming the screen beneath, so a screen that would be suspended again in the same pass is never remounted for nothing. Stale resume: a screen the stack resumes is checked against the store before it stands - a Live view whose ticket left the work cycle its screen stands on (ADR 0110) closes instead of resuming, and a resumed screen whose selected item left the factory's lists stands on the fact the store now answers, the same fallbacks the screen already derives today.

**Where preserved facts live, and what they are.** The stack's record stands in the shell, which is never suspended. No screen holds a second private copy of a fact the stack preserves: for the active screen, the stack's record is the one place its screen-local facts stand, and the screen reads and writes it through the interface. On suspend, the stack stores the screen's preserved facts; on resume, it hands them back, and the screen builds the rest of itself from the store. The preserved facts of a screen are its screen-local facts only - the facts the store does not hold:
- The Main view: the selection (which section the cursor stands in), the selected row of that section, the detail pane's scroll, the list filters (the Ticket filter and the Consultation history filter are views, not factory state), the focused pane, the sections' expansion states, the grouping folds (the grouping axis and the group order are factory state and come from the store, ADR 0058), the response draft and the response editor's standing, the Agent terminal's standing, and the presented facts of the Consultation detail: the stream lines, the session entries, the new-output mark, and the follow state.
- The Live view: the presented stream lines and the stale note (the Stale Agent output stands as a preserved fact, so a resume shows the last lines it showed), the body's scroll, and the ticket identity with its work cycle that make the screen's identity.
- The Consultation launcher: the form's draft, its slot values, and its replacement identity.
- The override panel: the pending override's ticket, its origin, and the form's values under the cursor's row.
- The decision, missing, close, safety, recovery, force, and delete panels: the body's scroll and the row under the cursor; their domain facts (the ticket's state, the turn log, the label facts) come from the store on resume.
- The repository select and repository init: the marked repositories' choices and the body's scroll; the init queue's remaining, ran, skipped, and refused counts are facts of the plane's in-flight work and stand in the shell, not in the panel.
- The Key guide and the Message view: their captured mode only; their content re-derives from the Control catalogue and the message history, which are facts of the plane and stand in the shell.

**Timers, reads, and spinner faces stop by unmount, decided by the module.** A screen's own subscriptions - the Consultation detail's body read, the Live view's stream read, the spinner faces' frame steps - live in the screen's tree, so the standard unmount cleanup stops them when the screen suspends, and the screen's first pass on resume re-reads and repopulates its presented facts from the preserved record, so the resumed frame stands as the suspended frame stood. The decision of which screen is active is made in one place, the stack module: the spec rejects the alternative in which each screen's hook asks an "active screen" fact whether to tick, because that scatters the stack's one decision across every screen's hooks and leaves a second, shallow seam into the stack that a screen can drift from. Locality: a screen's life is decided where its screen is mounted, and nothing else decides it. Leverage: the one rule - a suspended screen unmounts - stops every presentational subscription of every screen at once, the timers present today and the subscriptions any screen adds tomorrow, and stops them the same way the plane already stops the subscriptions of a closed screen.

**The key-ownership contract.** The active screen owns the keys; a suspended screen claims none. The plane's shell keeps its own base key subscription on the key bus through every transition, because the shell owns the emergency exit in every mode and the legacy keys of the base screen; while another screen is active it claims only the emergency exit, exactly as it does today, and its subscription's identity never changes, so a walk's snapshot of the key bus keeps its meaning across a suspend and resume of the base screen. Every other screen registers its key dispatch with the shared dispatch module under its own slot, the way it does today, and the shared dispatch module deepens one rule: the key bus holds one stable wrapper per screen slot, not per mounting of a screen. Suspend detaches the screen's wrappers from the key bus - a suspended screen holds no subscription at all, not an inert one - and resume re-attaches the same wrappers. A screen's close gives back its keys in the commit that draws the resumed frame, and the existing guard stands unchanged: a key landed in the window between a closed surface's last frame and its key release reaches the undrawn surface's wrapper and claims nothing, runs no behavior, and reports nothing. The stack adds no key of its own, moves no control, and changes no key: the Control catalogue stays the single owner of key meaning, and the Action bar and the Key keep naming exactly what they name today.

**The loops, the shell, and the theme.** The Observation cycle, the refresh schedule, and the Handoff dispatch are the plane's loops, owned by the shell under the named Loop policy (ADR 0129), and they run through every screen transition on the cadence the boot record names; the facts they and the screens share - the last polled agents, the Auto-handoff mode, the config, the message history - stand in the shell as standing facts, read by a screen on resume and written by the loops. The message line and the Action bar each screen stands on paint from those shell facts, so a screen resumed after a long suspend shows the factory's current state, not the state it had when it was suspended. Every screen, the base panes included, paints the Theme the environment resolved through the shared paint layer, and no screen holds a palette of its own (ADR 0024); the stack changes which screens paint and when, and how none of them paint. The two render sites stand as they stand: the production entry and the frame rig boot the same module from the same total Plane boot record (ADR 0129), and the stack is inside the module they both render.

## Testing Decisions

A good test for this deepening asserts external behavior only, through the frame rig's seam: the painted frame, the key bus through the rig's four kept reads, the runner's command list, and the facts a screen presents. It names a control from the Control catalogue and one outcome from the rig's closed set; it advances no state of the plane by hand; and it never reads the stack's record or a screen's interior to prove a fact the screen paints or the bus shows. The "suspended screen runs nothing" assertion is the pair of measurements the seam exposes: the runner's command list standing across a window longer than the suspended screen's tick period, and the painted frame standing across that same window; the resume assertion is the runner's list growing again and the resumed screen's presented facts moving on its first tick.

Modules and seams, in order of height:
- The screen stack module through the frame rig's seam (primary): open-suspend-resume cycles on the real plane - the guide over the Live view, a panel over the Main view's Consultation detail, the override over the Decision modal and over the Live view, the guide over the launcher - asserting frame equality across the suspension window, the runner's cadence, the preserved facts on resume through the painted frame, the stale resume (a work cycle left behind closes the Live view instead of resuming it), and the key-ownership contract through the rig's kept reads.
- The screen stack module through its own seam (secondary): a standing that plays a screen drives the transition rules and the preservation lifecycle - one entry per screen, the in-place fact update, replace without resume, the stale-resume close - against the module's interface, without a terminal.
- The shared dispatch module's slot-stable wrapper rule through the rig's kept reads: a suspended screen's wrapper is off the bus and back on it with the same identity after a resume; the stale-key guard's answer stands on the re-attached wrapper.
- The plane's loops through the Seeded plane's runner and clock: an Observation cycle tick lands during a screen suspension and its result shows on the resumed screen, while the runner's screen-local reads stand still.

Prior art the change must keep green, and how those suites assert: the Consultation frame suite opens the launcher, the guide, and the Consultation panels through the rig's named operations and asserts `opens` on the panel's title, `closes` on its going away, and `quiet` on the settled frame; the handoff frame suite opens the Live view on the live-view control, asserts the stream's title and the runner's visible-screen reads, closes on the cancel control, and asserts the base's spinner face on the resumed row; the auto-mode suite opens the Missing modal, the ticket close, and the decision surfaces and closes them through its close walk (the cancel control's key and a frame wait for the surface's text to leave); the action bar suite asserts the bar's packing in every mode the rig stands; the main view frame suite drives the guide's scroll window and reads the guide's rows through the rig's overlay-row probe, which keeps only the rows inside the overlay's borders and drops the screen around the box - a probe that measures exactly what the stack promises to leave unchanged. The frame rig's own suite pins the close proof and the stale-key guard through the four kept bus reads, and its architecture check holds the owned-surfaces floor (which grows and never shrinks: the new stack-behavior suites join it) and refuses any suite that reaches the key bus or a raw key outside the rig's escape list.

## Out of Scope

- Redesigning which screens exist, which controls a screen owns, or which key a control holds: the Control catalogue, the shared control library, and the shared chrome stand untouched, and the stack composes them without re-implementing any of them.
- The animation-frame paint work (ADR 0149, the frame module): the plane's paint still runs on the renderer's own clock until that lands, and this deepening does not change when the renderer draws. The two are complementary, and the interplay is one-way in the operator's favor: with the stack, a suspended screen's spinners and timers stop entirely, so once the frame module lands, the paint cadence of the plane is a function of the one active screen plus the plane's loops, never of the screens behind it.
- Changing the Action bar's hints, the Key guide's content, or any written word the operator reads; the deepening moves no control, changes no key, and rewords no hint.
- The screen-reader path and the light migration of the base panes, which stand open in the shared control's verification record; this deepening neither closes them nor moves them.
- Inherited theme contrast (ADR 0024): the stack changes which surfaces paint, and the inherited pairs remain unchecked the way they are.
- Preserving a screen's facts across a restart of the run: a restart starts the Main view fresh and empties the message history, as it does today.
- A screen that stays mounted but inert: an inert screen that keeps its subscriptions is the shallow form of this deepening, and the contract refuses it - a suspended screen holds no subscription at all.

## Further Notes

- The interaction with the frame module (ADR 0149) stands above: today the machine's cadence is the sum of every live screen's cadence, because every tick commits the whole tree; with the stack it is the active screen's cadence plus the loops' cadence. A suspended screen's spinner faces and timers stop entirely, so the frame module's paint work tunes one screen's drawing without racing the ticks of the screens behind it.
- The frame rig's census: the owned-surfaces floor grows by the new stack-behavior suites and, as always, never shrinks; the 193 waits on the key-ownership apparatus keep their shape and their meaning, because the four kept bus reads (`releaseKeys`, `keyBase`, `leftKeyHandler`, `staleKeyClaim`) stand unchanged and their meaning survives the base screen's suspend-resume only because of one production-side rule this deepening adds - the key bus holds one stable wrapper per screen slot. The committed walk that prints the rig's export count (`scripts/walk-frame-rig-counts.ts`) records the count at the head this lands: the deepening adds no new rig facts and no new rig operations, so the count stands or grows by the named operations the new suites take, never by a lower seam.
- The one fact the deepening refuses to hand out: a live "active screen" read for the rig. The rig already reads the active screen off the painted frame's markers - the full-screen surfaces' titles, the modal and panel titles' stable prefixes, the section boxes' focus markers, the detail panes' focus markers - and the deepening leaves that read exactly where it is. A named active-screen fact would open a new, lower seam: a test could then act on a screen the bar does not advertise, and the Action bar would stop being the fact the test reads.
- The Live view's keyed screen identity (the ticket identity with its work cycle, ADR 0110) is the plane's existing precedent for a screen that unmounts and remounts per cycle; the stack generalizes to every screen what the Live view already does for its cycles, and the stale-resume rule (close instead of resume when the stored fact left the store) is the stack form of the rule ADR 0110 decided for the work cycle.
