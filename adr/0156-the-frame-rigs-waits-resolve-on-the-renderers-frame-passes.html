ADR 0156: The frame rig's waits resolve on the renderer's frame passes ​

Status: accepted Date: 2026-11-14

Records that the frame rig's waits resolve on the renderer's frame passes instead of on a frame-equality shadow the rig samples on a wall-clock tick: its settle resolves when the renderer owes no further pass after the plane's asks have painted, and its frame waits resolve on the renderer's pass announcement as a one-shot promise, the frame poll standing behind under the same liveness-aware deadline. The wall-clock grace survives only as the rig's one named boot settle.

Context ​

Every wait the frame rig's named operations stand on resolves on a derived fact. The settle opens on a 30 ms wall-clock grace and then samples the renderer's painted buffer every 10 ms, accepting the frame when two consecutive samples read equal. The frame waits sample the same buffer on the same tick under the liveness-aware deadline ADR 0128 set: a 20 s window that holds for another window when it stood off the CPU for more than half, under a 50 s outer bound. The suite's census counts more than 3,200 wait sites across the frame suites, and the settle stands behind every key operation of them: a wait on a plane that owes no more paint costs the grace and two polls on top of the work the plane owed.

The fact the rig samples is a shadow of the renderer's state, and the rig pays for the blindness twice. A poll straddles a paint by a full poll interval: a paint that lands between two polls is read on the sample after it, one interval late. ADR 0128 paid for that class by doubling the deadline to 20 s; its census at the head that doubled it counted 875 frame waits, 1,888 hand-written wait descriptions, and 193 key-ownership waits, and the twelve flake issues of that class stand behind the doubled deadline. And the grace is the second payment of the same blindness: because the shadow cannot tell "the plane owes no more paint" from "the paint has not been sampled yet", the rig waits out wall time before it trusts the buffer, on every wait, not on the one that needs it.

The source facts already stand at the rig. The renderer announces each pass it paints, and it announces after it has written the buffer: the announcement names the pass, and the frame it names is the frame a pin can read. The rig already intercepts that announcement: it swallows the pass and re-announces it to the surfaces that register for it, so the announcement is a fact the rig already holds and already routes. And the renderer reports its own scheduling state on the renderer's public surface, whether a pass is owed, whether it is rendering, whether its frame loop is running. The same renderer class stands at both render sites, the production entry and the frame rig: the plane module they boot is one module, from one Plane boot record, and the renderer adapter ADR 0145 owns the production entry's renderer while the rig owns the test renderer. The renderer's test operations already stand on exactly these two facts: they await the pass announcement as a one-shot promise, and they fall back on the renderer's own idle report when the frame loop is down.

Two landed records absorbed part of this candidate, and this record scopes to what remains. ADR 0128 moved the rig's deadline to the liveness-aware window, so a descheduled worker stretches a wait instead of failing it; that decision stands, and this record keeps the window, the hold, and the outer bound. ADR 0149 took the per-screen animation clocks out of the frame stream, so the tick source of the plane's animation frames is a fact the renderer's frame facts already carry, and the test renderer runs no tick of its own. What remains is the resolve rule: the waits stand on the renderer's frame facts instead of the polled shadow, and the grace survives only in the boot window.

Decision ​

The frame rig's waits resolve on the renderer's frame facts: the pass announcement and the renderer's report of its own scheduling state. The rig stops resolving its waits on the frame-equality shadow sampled on the wall-clock tick, and it reads the renderer's own report and awaits the renderer's own announcement instead. The deepening is one seam: the wait and the paint meet at the renderer's pass announcement, and the rig asks the renderer for the two facts it resolves on. That is the locality the shadow never had, the rig re-deriving by sampling what the renderer reports, and the leverage stands on the interface: two facts and a deadline serve the suite's more than 3,200 wait sites the way the one deadline already serves them.

  • The settle: after the rig has run the operation, it settles the passive work the renderer owes, reads the renderer's report, and while the renderer owes a pass it awaits the renderer's next pass announcement as a one-shot promise; on each pass it settles the passive work again and re-reads the report. It resolves with the frame the renderer last painted when the renderer owes nothing more, and a settle that runs after an operation that owes no paint resolves on the frame that stands, with no pass owed after it. While a face steps on the frame module's tick port (ADR 0149), the renderer owes a pass only between ticks, and the settle resolves in the gap, the same behavior the equal-frame settle shows today.

  • The frame waits: the wait attaches a one-shot promise to the renderer's pass announcement. On each announcement the pin reads the buffer the pass painted; the announcement names the pass, and it comes after the renderer has written the buffer, so the frame the announcement names is the frame the pin reads. The pin stands as it stands today, a read of the painted buffer, and the wait resolves on the frame the pin accepts. The suites' pins keep their meaning, and they resolve earlier.

  • The frame poll stands behind under the same liveness-aware deadline. It witnesses a renderer that paints without announcing, and it measures the off-schedule time the liveness-aware deadline stands on; a bare one-shot promise has no such witness. The deadlines stand as ADR 0128 set them, the 20 s window that holds for another window when it stood off the CPU for more than half, the 50 s outer bound, and the wait fails on the last painted frame. A wait that never gets its pass fails on the deadline, never a hang.

  • The resolve rule reads no stack and counts no ask. The renderer's scheduling report is the renderer's own authority over what it owes, and a count the rig kept of the asks it intercepted would read a quiescence that is not there where an ask path schedules without the public ask. The stack-trace classification of render asks stands confined to the rig's two render-ask holds, the hold that lets the plane's asks through and the hold that swallows every ask, whose contract needs the plane-versus-internal distinction.

  • The wall-clock grace survives only as the rig's one named boot settle. It covers the plane's boot under the held loop policy: the one-shot boot read, the initial-load settle ADR 0154's refresh coordinator stands on, and the first probe that runs on the settle, and their first paints. The boot is the one window in which a plane fact is not yet a renderer fact: before the plane's first pass, the plane can owe a paint it has not yet asked the renderer for, and the renderer's owed-pass report is not armed over that window. The named grace covers that window once per boot, and after it the boot settle stands on the renderer's frame facts the way every other settle does. Every settle outside the boot window holds no wall grace.

  • The rig's interface stands unchanged. The operations keep their names, their fields, their named outcomes, the immediate press, and the key-ownership window; the interface is two renderer facts and a deadline, and the implementation is the announcement, the report, the passive-work settle, and the poll standing behind. The owned surface grows and never shrinks: the one new named surface, the boot settle, joins it, and the rig's own architecture test and the campaign's counter walk police it the way they police it today.

  • The decision is order-safe against the frame module (ADR 0149) and the renderer adapter (ADR 0145) in either direction. The waits stand on the renderer's frame facts whatever tick source the plane's animation frames come from, the test renderer runs no tick of its own, and each side of the seam owns its own renderer: the rig owns the test renderer, and the production entry owns the production renderer, through the renderer adapter once it lands. The decision touches the test render site alone.

Considered options ​

  • Keep the polled shadow (status quo): the poll keeps sampling the buffer's stability on the wall tick, the grace stays on every wait, and the straddle stays behind the doubled deadline. It loses because the renderer already announces the fact the shadow re-derives, and the rig already intercepts the announcement for the surfaces; keeping the shadow keeps both payments of the blindness, the straddle ADR 0128 paid for with the deadline and the grace every wait pays.
  • Resolve only on the renderer's pass announcements and retire the poll: it loses because the renderer announces a pass only to the listeners it has, and a renderer that paints through a path the rig did not intercept would leave the wait to fail on the deadline with the pin never having read the paint. The poll standing behind keeps the pin honest, and it keeps the off-schedule measurement the liveness-aware deadline stands on; retire the poll and the window loses its witness.
  • Count the rig's own intercepted asks: a wrapper on the renderer's ask reports outstanding asks instead of the renderer's own scheduling report. It loses because that is the shadow again: the rig re-derives the renderer's private scheduling state from its own interception, and an ask path that schedules without the public ask reads a quiescence that is not there. The renderer is the authority over what it owes, and it reports it.
  • Announced-pass frame equality: keep the two-equal-frames rule, but take the frames on the pass announcements instead of on the wall tick. It loses because equality is a test of the buffer's contents, not of the plane's obligation: a face stepping on its 100 ms step makes equal frames impossible to observe across the step, so the rule still waits on wall time between passes, and a pass that changes one glyph waits out the gap before the rig learns the plane owes nothing. The renderer's own owed-pass report is the fact the equality test approximates, and the report stands on the renderer's public surface.

Consequences ​

  • The operator sees no change: the decision stands on the test render site alone. The plane the operator runs keeps its renderer, its entry, its frame module, and its screens, and the operator's share is the frame suite's wall time, which the rig no longer spends on sampling.
  • The machine: a wait costs the passes the plane owes and nothing else. A wait that owes no paint costs no grace, and a paint that lands mid-wait is caught on the pass it lands on, not on the deadline it outruns. The rig's waits and the renderer's test operations stand on the same two facts, so the rig and the renderer it tests read the same thing.
  • The test: the frame suites keep every pin and wait earlier. The rig's own frame suite, head of the owned list, pins the new resolve rule: the settle on a pass and on no pass owed, the frame wait on the announcement, the poll under the deadline when the renderer announces nothing, the deadline's failure on a renderer that owes no pass and announces none, and the boot settle's grace over the one-shot boot read and the initial-load settle. The rig's own architecture test keeps pinning the owned surface, the floor growing and never shrinking, the retired surface, and the shape of the waits, and the campaign's counter walk keeps pinning the rig's exports.
  • The glossary: the Frame rig entry is rewritten to name the renderer's frame pass as the fact the rig's waits stand on, the boot settle as the one place the wall-clock grace stands, and the frame poll as the witness standing behind under the same deadline. This record lands with the implementation, not with this ADR.
