ADR 0145: The renderer adapter owns the repaint policy ​

Status: accepted Date: 2026-11-13

Records that the plane's entry module stops marking the renderer's private full-repaint flag on every frame and instead hands the frame's facts to a renderer adapter module that owns the renderer and the repaint policy. The policy answers how much of the screen each frame rewrites: the default is the renderer's native diff, which writes only the cells that changed, and a full rewrite stands when the frame's facts say the renderer's model of the screen can be wrong, on a terminal resize, on output that lands on the terminal outside the renderer, and on a detected byte-loss signal. Until a byte-loss signal exists, the entry module keeps passing the blanket force as an explicit policy input on every frame, so the screen keeps its recorded insurance against the drift class of OpenTUI issue 1187 and the operator reads the same screen as today. The decision lands in one step: the adapter with the full policy stands now, and the lift of the blanket force is the entry module's withdrawal of one fact, not a redesign of the policy.

Context ​

The entry module constructs the native renderer from OpenTUI (installed 0.5.11) and installs a frame callback on it. The callback marks the renderer's private full-repaint flag on every frame, so every frame rewrites the whole screen. The renderer otherwise diffs a frame against its own model of the screen: it marks a model cell as written when it emits the cell's bytes. If the host terminal loses the bytes of a frame, the model and the screen drift apart, and the later diffed frames skip the lost cells, so a stale fragment of an earlier frame stands on the screen until the next full rewrite. The blanket force overwrites any lost byte within one frame, and the screen always converges to the model. It is the recorded workaround for the drift class of OpenTUI issue 1187.

The price of the blanket force multiplies on every screen change the plane makes. A spinner tick every 100 ms while a ticket wears the Starting window, the Observation cycle's report every 5 s, the Consultation view's body refresh and the Live view's stream line every 1 s, the modal pop-in's frames every 16 ms, and a key press each rewrite the entire terminal, and the renderer's diff path, which writes only the cells that changed, never runs in a production run. Every screen the plane adds, and every fix that touches how the screen paints, pays that price again: the lever that would make a tick cost one glyph is held down, so no change to the paint buys back the difference.

The verification record of the shared controls measures what the force does and does not buy, on the installed renderer and the terminals it names (Bun 1.4.0, tmux 3.7a, 40 s runs: 0.5.9 lost 19 to 30 captures per run, 0.5.11 lost 0 to 30): the byte records place the measured corruption in the renderer's own frames, not in the transport, and the measured form of the failure matches the stale-buffer class of OpenTUI issue 1212, a native defect the force neither causes nor cures, and the record's standing toggle of the force leaves the rate inside the baseline range. Two defect classes stand, then: the 1187 drift class, in which lost bytes falsify the model and only a full rewrite heals it, which the force insures, and the 1212 stale-buffer class, in which the renderer's own frame carries the corruption, which it does not. No detector of the drift class stands in the installed renderer or in the terminals: the plane cannot tell a drifted model from a sound one, and the blanket force is the answer it gives to a question it cannot ask.

The seam where the cost stands is shallow. The entry module marks a private renderer field per frame and owns no policy: it cannot tell a frame in which the model may be wrong from a frame in which one cell changed, and the knowledge of when the screen model can be wrong has no module. The UI module paints its facts into the renderer and asks for renders; it stands on neither side of the seam between the plane's commits and the screen's bytes, and screen-model correctness rides on a per-frame callback in the entry module that the UI module's commits unknowingly trigger. The renderer already forces a full rewrite on the frames where it rebuilds its model, the resize, the capability answer, the resume from suspend, on its own; the entry's blanket force is the only force on the frames the renderer would otherwise diff.

Decision ​

A renderer adapter module owns the renderer and the repaint policy. It constructs the renderer from the entry module's boot facts, the terminal and the renderer options the entry stands today, it installs the frame callback that runs the policy, it reads the renderer's own events, the resize and the external output the renderer detects, as frame facts, and it stands in the entry module's shutdown order, so the renderer returns the terminal before the run ends. The entry module keeps the construction of the App and its mount on the renderer, the herdr identity report before the first frame, and the run's shutdown order, and it hands the adapter the frame facts it alone can know: the writes the plane makes on the terminal outside the renderer, a byte-loss signal when one exists, and the blanket force until a byte-loss signal exists.

The policy is a function of the frame's facts, and it answers how much of the screen the frame rewrites:

  • The default is the renderer's native diff: the frame writes only the cells that changed.
  • A terminal resize forces a full rewrite. The renderer already rebuilds its model on the resize and answers it with a forced rewrite on its own, and the adapter observes the renderer's resize event, so the fact stands in the policy's set and the answer to when the model's trust is rebuilt stands in one module.
  • Output that lands on the terminal outside the renderer and could paint or move a cell forces a full rewrite of the next frame: the renderer's model does not know the bytes an outside writer put on the screen, and a diff would skip the cells the writer touched. The plane's own bell write on a Fault paints no cell and moves no cursor, so it forces no rewrite, and the entry module reports it to the adapter as the fact it is.
  • A detected byte-loss signal forces a full rewrite for the frames it covers, so the lost cells are overwritten within one frame, the way the blanket force did. No such signal stands in the installed renderer or in the terminals the verification record names. That is the honest edge of the decision: while no signal stands, the plane cannot tell a drifted model from a sound one, and the policy trusts no diff it cannot check.
  • Until a byte-loss signal exists, the blanket force stands as an explicit policy input the entry module passes on every frame. The policy's answer is a full rewrite on every frame, the screen behaves exactly as it does today on the drift class of issue 1187, and the private field the entry module marked on every frame stands now as the one private the adapter module owns, set only when the policy answers full.

The decision lands in one step: the adapter with the full policy stands now, and the diff branch is testable on the day it lands, because the policy is a function the developer can feed with frame facts. The two-step shape, an adapter now whose policy always forces, and a second change that lifts the force after the signal is verified, is rejected: it ships a policy that is not yet a policy, it records this decision's risk analysis twice, and it makes the lift a redesign of the very module this decision builds. The lift is cheaper when the fact is already named: the entry module stops passing the blanket force and hands the signal over, and the policy, which already answers a byte-loss signal like any other force fact, is unchanged by the lift.

The UI module is untouched by the move. It keeps requesting renders and painting its facts into the renderer, and it stops being responsible for the screen model's correctness: today that responsibility stands unnamed in the entry module's per-frame callback, which every commit of the UI module triggers; after the move it stands named, in the adapter's policy. The depth is the point of the seam: the private renderer field, a leak of the renderer's internals, is owned by the one module that stands between the plane and the renderer, and the rest of the plane, the entry module included, never sees it.

Considered options ​

  • Keep the blanket force in the entry module's frame callback, permanently: the screen keeps converging within one frame of a lost byte, and the plane keeps paying for every commit a full rewrite of the terminal, and the private field marking stays where the policy that justifies it is not written. When a byte-loss signal arrives, the plane would redesign the very seam this decision moves, and the entry module would keep owning a fact it cannot read, the state of the terminal's transport.
  • Diff without a byte-loss signal: lift the force now and trust the renderer's diff on every frame. The renderer's model of the screen is exactly what a lost byte falsifies, and no fact would tell the policy the model is wrong, so the diffed frames keep skipping the lost cells and the stale fragment stands until any full rewrite, which the diff-only policy schedules rarely. The operator reads that screen as the plane's own facts, and the plane does not take an unobserved screen for a correct one.
  • Move the policy into the UI module instead of the entry-level adapter: the UI module knows the screen's content, but it knows nothing of the renderer's model of the screen, of the terminal's transport, or of the writes that land on the terminal outside the renderer. The policy's facts are facts the UI module cannot read, so placing the policy there would make a module own a decision it cannot make, and the seam would move from the renderer's edge to the middle of the paint, further from the facts than it stands now.

Consequences ​

  • The screen the operator reads is unchanged while the blanket force stands: every frame still rewrites the whole screen, the frame tests read the same painted frames, and the drift class of issue 1187 keeps its insurance. What moves is where the decision stands: a policy of explicit frame facts in the adapter module, instead of a private field the entry module marks on every frame.
  • The leverage pays out at the lift. When the entry module withdraws the blanket force, a spinner tick writes one glyph, the Observation cycle's report writes the rows that moved, the Consultation view's refresh and the Live view's stream write their changed lines, the pop-in writes its transform, and a key press writes its effect, and every screen change the plane builds after that pays per changed cell instead of per screen.
  • The risk that stands after the lift is the one the force insures: a byte loss of the drift class of issue 1187 that no signal reports. The screen then converges on the next forced rewrite, which the policy schedules on the resize and on outside output that could paint a cell, and it stands open between them. The stale-buffer class of issue 1212 is a separate native defect the force never cured and this move does not worsen; the verification record keeps it open beside the drift class.
  • The exit condition for the blanket force: it lifts when a byte-loss signal exists and stands verified on the plane's own screens. The signal is a detection of the drift class of issue 1187 in a release of OpenTUI the plane installs, or a terminal-level probe the plane can read; the lift is not the signal alone, a terminal walk on the installed renderer and on the terminals the verification record names must show a diffed frame healing a lost byte within one frame and no stale row standing behind a selection. Until both stand, the entry module keeps passing the blanket force, and the policy's diff branch is the path the walk will prove before it becomes the production path.
  • A terminal walk must re-verify after the move, with the blanket force still standing, that the handoff to the adapter broke no paint: the spinner face beside its word in the Starting window, the Observation cycle's report on the list and the section headers' counts, the Consultation view's body and the Live view's stream under a working agent on each Agent host, a modal pop-in settling on its final body, a resize of the terminal mid-stream, the plane's bell on a Fault, and a selection over the screen, the act the 1187 and 1212 artifacts both showed through. After the lift, the walk adds the drift class itself, a diffed frame on the installed terminals with the recorded renderer version beside the recorded terminal, and the record joins the verification record of the shared controls.
