ADR 0159: The plane stands the key owner as a fact the rig's key windows read ​

Status: accepted

Date: 2026-11-14

Records that the plane stands the key owner as a fact the rig's key windows read: which surface of the plane owns the keys, or no surface. The owner is the shell's own dispatch fact, the active Interaction mode, or no surface, and the plane stands it the way it already stands the shell's other facts. The rig's key ownership windows resolve on the fact, keep their names and named outcomes per ADR 0156, and retire the bus walk from the windows' path where the fact names the owner completely. The rig keeps on the key bus what the fact cannot say: the registrations the bus still holds after a close, and the stale-claim dispatch to the handler a closed surface left there.

Context ​

The operator sees nothing, and the cost of the walk is the rig's. The rig's key ownership windows are its own waits, not the operator's: after a walk opens or closes a surface, the rig waits for the surface's key ownership to settle with it, and for a closed surface to give the keys back. ADR 0128 counted the windows in the census it keeps: 193 key- ownership waits beside 875 frame waits, under the one liveness-aware deadline of a 20 s window that holds for another window when the runner is off schedule, under a 50 s outer bound. The owner the windows wait on is a fact about the plane, and today the plane does not state it to the rig.

Today the rig finds the owner by walking the renderer's key bus. A second polling loop stands beside the frame poll, at its 10 ms cadence, and reads the bus's subscription list until it names the surface that owns the keys. A standing watcher on the bus's listener registration witnesses a surface take the keys even when it gives them back before the wait's first poll. The base snapshot, the left-behind handler read, and the stale-claim dispatch keep a press from landing on a surface that no longer owns the keys: the walk's Enter, dispatched to the handler a closed surface left on the bus, must be refused by the surface's guard, which runs no behavior and reports nothing (issue #317). The walk works, and it is the rig's knowledge of the bus's structure, not the plane's knowledge of itself.

The owner the walk walks to is the shell's own dispatch fact. The shell routes every key by a rule it already runs: a standing overlay - the Consultation launcher, the utility views, the override panel, the action modals - owns its keys, and the shell keeps only the emergency exit; the response editor of an open detail owns every other key; the Agent terminal owns its keys; and the Main view's active screen answers through the Control catalogue on the shared dispatch hook every surface uses. The owner is the active Interaction mode, or no surface: the active screen of the screen stack, an open modal, the open detail, the Consultation launcher, the override panel, the Agent terminal, the Key guide, the Message view, or nothing standing. The key bus stands on the renderer, the renderer the rig owns under test, and the production entry's renderer stands beside it through the renderer adapter of ADR 0145. The walk crosses down into the renderer's internal structure to learn a fact the shell already knows.

The walk is a shallow stand for a deep fact. Its knowledge lives in the rig: the rig knows how the bus registers a handler, how it announces a new listener, and how it compares handlers by identity, and every change to the shell's dispatch that moves ownership can only be pinned by the rig re-walking the bus. The plane knows the owner the moment the shell's dispatch state settles, because the owner is that state under the rule its dispatch runs. ADR 0156 stands the frame-pass resolution around the windows and keeps their names and named outcomes; ADR 0157 remounts the App per test on the warm renderer, and the fact must stand on the mounted App's surface state the pool resets. The walk itself is the seam this record settles.

Decision ​

  • The plane stands the key owner as a readable fact: which surface of the plane owns the keys, or no surface. The owner is the shell's own dispatch fact, the active Interaction mode, or no surface: the active screen of the screen stack, an open modal, the open detail, the Consultation launcher, the override panel, the Agent terminal, the Key guide, the Message view, or no surface standing. The shell computes the fact from its own dispatch state, by the same rule its key dispatch runs to route the key, and it stands the fact on the plane the way the plane already stands the shell's other facts: the mode the Action bar states, the hints the bar carries, and the surface the frame names. The Seeded plane record stands the fact beside the facts it already holds.
  • The rig's key ownership windows keep their names and their named outcomes, per ADR 0156: opens, closes, refuses, shows, hides, moved, quiet. A window's body checks the fact on the renderer's frame passes once ADR 0156 lands, and on the rig's standing poll until then, and it ends when the fact names the surface the window wants. The poll that reads the fact runs the same settle in its own turn that the frame poll already runs: the flush a commit queues lands on the scheduler's clock, not on any frame (issue #311), and a fact read before the flush lands would settle on the owner the commit was about to move.
  • The second polling loop the rig ran beside the frame poll, walking the bus's subscription list to find the owner, retires from the windows' path, and one loop stands where two stood. The standing watcher on the bus's listener registration retires as the owner witness, and the plane's record takes its place: it stands the owners the fact has stood since a named snapshot, so a surface that took the keys and gave them back before the wait's first read still leaves its mark, and a walk that never stood the opened surface's owner has no release to wait for. The release window's "proved nothing" refusal reads that standing and says so out loud, as it does today.
  • The bus walk retires from the windows' path where the fact names the owner completely. What the fact cannot say stays named and stays on the key bus: the fact settles on the shell's dispatch state, and it projects neither the registrations the renderer's key bus still holds after a close, nor what a key delivered to a registration the plane no longer routes will do. Between the fallback frame and the close's cleanup landing, the closed surface's handler can still stand on the bus, and a key delivered there is the stale claim the surface's guard must refuse. The rig keeps the base snapshot, the left-behind handler read, and the stale-claim dispatch, the walk's Enter to the left-behind handler, whose refusal the guard answers.
  • The key owner fact is a test-side projection on the plane. The production shell's key dispatch stands exactly as it stands: the shell keeps the keys, the paint, and the screen stack per ADR 0155, and no production interface gains a field for the test's benefit. The shell's dispatch hook, its report sinks, and the renderer's key bus stand unchanged. The record moves the rig's discovery cost, and nothing the operator drives.
  • Under the warm rig pool of ADR 0157, the owner fact resets per test with the App's tree, which the pool remounts per test on the warm renderer. The pool's warm pieces, the test renderer with its terminal and its screen model, the App's root, and the frame event witness, hold no dispatch state and no registration of the predecessor: the unmount runs every surface's cleanup, and a window under the pool reads the current test's owner, never the predecessor's. A test that takes no pool reads the fact cold, exactly as its bus walk stands today.
  • This decision is order-safe with ADR 0156 and ADR 0157 in both directions. Both are recorded on this tree and not yet implemented; their issues are open. If ADR 0156 lands first, the windows' checks run on the renderer's frame passes and read the fact there. If this record lands first, ADR 0156's frame-pass resolution applies to the same windows unchanged, and the fact stands on the mounted App's surface state, which ADR 0157's pool remounts per test. ADR 0158's static key and hint table stands on the catalogue and the mode, and the owner fact stands beside it: the table is static per setup, and the owner changes mid-test. ADR 0150's screen stack, when it lands, stands the owner's base surface, the active screen the stack holds.
  • The owned surface grows and never shrinks. The architecture test's closed floor and counter walk stand, and the fact is one more owned fact beside the facts the plane already stands.
  • The deletion test: deleting the fact returns the walk to the rig. The windows read the renderer's key bus structure again, the second polling loop stands beside the frame poll, the standing watcher stands on the bus's listener registration, and the knowledge of what owns the keys stands in the rig instead of at the seam.

Considered options ​

  • Keep the rig's bus walk and watcher (status quo): the poll keeps walking the subscription list at its 10 ms cadence beside the frame poll, and the watcher keeps witnessing the registration. Rejected: it stands a second polling loop per key operation across the 193 windows ADR 0128 counted, it keeps the discovery knowledge, what owns the keys, as the rig's knowledge of the renderer's internal structure, and every change to the shell's dispatch that moves ownership can only be pinned by the rig re-walking the bus, where a pin on the plane's fact would stand.
  • Let the rig watch the bus's listener registration instead of polling it: the standing watcher alone carries the windows, and the poll retires. Rejected: the rig still knows the bus's structure, the coupling moves from a poll to a subscription, the ADR 0128 census counts the windows either way, and the plane's fact, which the shell already computes to dispatch the key, is never stood at the seam the frame suites stand on.
  • Stand the key owner as a production report sink of the shell, beside the list and status lines ADR 0155 names for the boot sequence module. Rejected: the report sinks are the plane's reports into the shell's paint, a production interface the test's fact borrows through; the plane is the seam the frame suites stand on, and the fact belongs on it, which keeps the production interface exactly as it stands.
  • Let each key operation re-derive ownership from the frame it just read: the window parses the painted surface from the pixels of the last pass and ends when the pixels name the surface it wants. Rejected: the frame is the paint's output, and reading ownership back out of painted pixels is a walk of the paint, not of the dispatch; the fact stands one layer deeper than the pixels, in the shell's own dispatch state.

Consequences ​

  • The operator sees no change: the shell's key dispatch stands exactly as it stands, the operator's keys reach the same surfaces in the same order, and the emergency exit answers from every surface as it does today. The fact stands on the plane behind the frame the operator sees, and it changes nothing the operator drives.
  • The machine: the rig runs one polling loop where it ran two. The windows' checks read the plane's fact on the rig's standing poll, and on the renderer's frame passes once ADR 0156 lands, instead of walking the renderer's key bus beside the frame poll. The discovery knowledge, what owns the keys, stands at the seam instead of in the rig: the plane is the deep module that knows its own dispatch state, and the rig is the shallow caller that reads the fact. A change to the shell's dispatch that moves ownership pins at the plane's fact, and the rig's leverage over the plane's behavior grows through the seam instead of through the bus's structure.
  • The test: the frame suites pin the windows' named outcomes through the fact, the same outcomes ADR 0128's census counted, opens, closes, refuses, shows, hides, moved, quiet. A test author names a surface and an outcome, and the window's check reads the plane's standing of the owner beside the owners it stood since the snapshot. Under the warm rig pool the window reads the current test's owner: the App's tree remounts per test, and the predecessor's owner cannot stand warm. The architecture test's closed floor and counter walk stand, and the frame suites' call sites name no new key, no new predicate, and no new deadline.
  • The test: the stale-claim check stands on the key bus as before. The fact settles on the dispatch state and says nothing the rig needs it to say about the registrations the bus still holds, so the rig keeps the base snapshot, the left-behind handler read, and the stale-claim dispatch, and a press still cannot land on a surface that no longer owns the keys.
  • The glossary: the Interaction mode entry gains the fact, the plane stands the active Interaction mode as a fact the rig's key windows read, and the Frame rig entry's line on a surface's key ownership moves from the bus walk to the fact. Both entries land with the implementation, not with this ADR.
